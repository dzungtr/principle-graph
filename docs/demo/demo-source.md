# Demo source: demand, prices, and interest rates

When households and businesses spend more, demand for goods and services rises.
If production does not increase at the same pace, prices tend to rise.

Higher interest rates make borrowing more expensive. More expensive borrowing
can reduce spending by households and investment by businesses. Reduced demand
can ease upward pressure on prices, although the effect depends on how strongly
spending responds to borrowing costs.

The relationship is not mechanical: supply disruptions can raise prices even
when demand is weak. A query about rising interest rates should therefore return
both the demand-reduction path and the supply-disruption qualification.
